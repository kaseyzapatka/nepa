[Fact Sheet 1: NEPA by the Numbers — New Insights on Environmental Reviews for Energy Infrastructure](factsheet1_key_insights.qmd)

- Title, authors (Kasey Zapatka, Kelsey Landau), contributors

- Key Findings (callout box)

  - 91% of energy-related federal actions are categorically excluded
  - Programmatic/tiered reviews are rare but demonstrably faster
  - Department of the Interior is the interagency collaboration hub, with Defense and Energy
    as key partners
  - FRA (2023) page limits show a real, but modest, effect on EIS length
  - Data quality/completeness is a structural limitation

- Executive Summary

  - Partnership context (CATF + Kasey Zapatka, NEPATEC 2.0 / PNNL PermitAI)
  - First phase of a planned series

- Recommendations

  - Invest in NEPA review data management/transparency, incl. publishing CE determinations
    across agencies
  - Increase agency use of programmatic and tiered reviews
  - Strengthen DOI/DOD/DOE coordination capacity
  - Invest in federal metadata standards, document-linkage practices, centralized submission
    (PermitAI, CEQ NEPA Data and Technology Standard, ePermit Act)

- Definitions

  - Categorical exclusion (CE), Environmental assessment (EA), Environmental impact statement
    (EIS), Programmatic review, Tiered review, Decarbonization technology

- Background

  - NEPA overview and the permitting-reform debate
  - The NEPA data challenge (agency data management practices, bespoke research subsets)
  - A fresh perspective on NEPA (NEPATEC 2.0 / PNNL PermitAI, CATF–Zapatka partnership)

- Finding 1: The NEPA process is categorically excluding small-scale projects and scrutinizing
  the impacts of larger ones

  - 1.1 The vast majority of energy-related federal actions do not require EAs or EISs
    (Figure 1: all-energy process composition; Figure 2: DOE/BLM process composition)
  - 1.2 Solar reviews are faster than decarbonization reviews overall, and review intensity
    correlates with generation capacity (Figure 3: solar timeline distribution; Figure 4: solar
    capacity distribution by process type)

- Finding 2: Effective agency implementation of existing NEPA options can speed review times

  - 2.1 Programmatic and tiered reviews remain underutilized strategic tools (Figure 5: review
    duration by review type and process)
  - 2.2 Interagency coordination could meaningfully impact efficiency — bridge-score analysis,
    DOI/DOD/DOE (Figure 6: bridge-score ranking; Figure 7: cross-department flow/Sankey diagram)

- Finding 3: FRA (2023) page limits are associated with a real, but modest, reduction in page
  lengths

  - Page-length trend over time (Figure 8)

- Caveats and Considerations

  - Data quality and completeness remain a structural limitation
  - High CE usage is not automatically a normative success
  - Faster timelines are not the only policy goal
  - Some important questions are out of scope for this first phase
